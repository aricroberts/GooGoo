The problem with the current build
A great plan, stuck being one person's plan

The 100-day tracker is genuinely good product work — phased structure, daily specificity, a clean completion loop. But three things cap how far it can travel as-is.

01

It's written for one body

Every day's workout is hand-authored for a 49-year-old, 220 lb, chest-and-cardio goal. Anyone else opening the app gets someone else's plan.

02

Progress lives in a browser

A random UUID in localStorage is the only identity. Clear your cache, switch phones, or reinstall — the streak and the history are gone.

03

No reason to come back tomorrow

Nothing nudges a return visit, celebrates a streak, or shows the trend line. Completion is silent — the app never tells you you're winning.

What an account layer buys you
Three features, one login

Each pillar below maps directly onto something the current codebase already half-has — this is extension, not a rewrite.

◆

Generated, not authored

A short intake (goal, equipment, days/week, injuries) replaces the hardcoded day map with a produced one — same day-card format, different content per person.

  • Quiz → plan in under a minute
  • Regenerate a phase without losing history
  • Swap a single day if a machine's taken
◆

An account, not a UUID

Email or social sign-in replaces the anonymous localStorage ID. The Supabase table already keyed by user_id needs almost no schema change to become multi-tenant.

  • Sign in on any phone, same plan
  • One account, multiple plans over time
  • Export or delete your data on request
◆

Progress with memory

Completed days already exist as data — accounts make them worth looking at: streaks, phase trends, and a record that outlives any single 100-day run.

  • Streak counter and weekly consistency
  • Waist / weight check-ins charted over time
  • Plan two starts where plan one ended
Why a subscription has to earn its keep
A static plan is a PDF. A paid one has to change its mind.

Nobody keeps paying $7 a month for a calendar that doesn't notice them. For now, the adaptive engine runs on a 60-second manual check-in — no wearable required — and rewrites the week ahead from what you report. Not the whole 100 days, just what's next.

Roadmap — not in the first build
Manual inputs prove the model. Wearables scale it later.

The adaptive engine doesn't care where its four numbers come from — a check-in form and a synced Watch feed it the same shape of data. Shipping on manual input first means no HealthKit review, no OAuth scopes, no Android/iOS parity work before the core loop is validated. Once the adaptation logic is proven on self-report, swapping in automatic sync is additive, not a rebuild.

⌚

Apple Health / Apple Watch — later

Would read workout sessions, active calories, and resting heart rate through HealthKit, so a finished chest day auto-marks complete instead of waiting on a check-in. Not required to launch or to validate the adaptive logic.

⌚

Google Fit / Wear OS — later

Same shape on Android via the Sessions and Recording APIs. Both plug into the same verdict logic the check-in widget above already runs — sync just replaces four typed numbers with four synced ones.
